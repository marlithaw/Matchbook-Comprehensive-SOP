Culture & Student Support Playbook

Living Document — Review Annually

This playbook is organized the way the work actually flows — from the daily operational rhythm outward to the systems that govern each domain. Use it as a reference, a training tool for the team, and a compliance anchor.

North Star: This role is not discipline-driven. It is systems-driven, relationship-driven, and prevention-focused — removing barriers to learning so every student has the behavioral, social-emotional, attendance, safety, and family supports necessary to remain engaged in instruction and thrive.
Part One

Role Architecture

The Director of Student Support and Culture is the systems architect and people champion for every student who needs more than the classroom can give — sitting at the intersection of behavior/culture, attendance/family, safety/compliance, special populations, student wellness, and team leadership.

Part Three

Behavior MTSS System

Matchbook's structured continuum is preventative, restorative, data-driven, and family-partnered.

Critical ruleA tier is only complete when it is documented. An undocumented intervention is not an intervention.

Tier 1 — Universal (4 weeks)

Gatekeeper checklist before any Tier 2 consideration: taught expectations, documented classroom redirection, PowerSchool logs, teacher-initiated parent contact. Gatekeeper: the designated administrator verifies and signs off.

Tier 2 — Targeted (6 weeks)

DBRC implemented for 6 weeks minimum with an assigned mentor and weekly data review. A parent meeting must occur before initiating a DBRC — it is never a default escalation shortcut. Exit: averages a 4 consistently for 6–8 weeks.

Tier 3 — Intensive (6-week cycles)

Continued DBRC with skills component, Behavior Contract or Critical Behavior Contract, Aspire behavior planning, individualized BSP, BIP if warranted. FBA only after Tier 1/2 fidelity is documented — a team decision, not administrative.

DBRC Daily Workflow

Teacher rates each period → brief conference → electronic entry before leaving the building → mentor reviews with the student → dashboard updated by 3:00 PM Monday → Dean enters ISS/OSS data.

Part Four

Attendance System

Attendance is a wellness indicator, not just compliance. Chronic absenteeism is missing 10% or more of school days. Every absence prompts one question: Is this willful or barrier-based?

5 days
Letter sent home — attendance clerk / Director, logged in PowerSchool.
7 days
Email + ParentSquare message from the Director.
7–8 days
Parent phone call from the Director, with call summary logged.
9–10 days
Parent home visit coordinated by Director + counselor.
10+ days
Certified letter sent home, copy retained in file.
No response
DCS referral for educational neglect, led by the School Counselor with Director support.

Transportation and housing barriers route to McKinney-Vento; medical concerns route to wellness support; willful avoidance routes to the MTSS behavior framework. Attendance Success Agreements are formal written commitments — developed collaboratively, reviewed on a set interval, and linked to identified barriers and specific school support commitments.

Part Five

Student Wellness & Safety

Threat Assessment Protocol

  • Remove the student to a supervised, calm space; gather facts without interrogating.
  • Convene the team (Director, Counselor, Dean, IPS Police as warranted) the same day.
  • Determine threat level; develop a Safety Plan if warranted; document within 24 hours.

Suicide Ideation Protocol

  • Immediately notify the counselor and Director; never leave the student alone.
  • Counselor conducts a screening (Columbia Protocol); same-day parent notification required.
  • If imminent risk, call 911 and IPS Police; Safety Plan and re-entry meeting required before return.

DCS Reporting

Any employee who suspects abuse or neglect notifies a school counselor immediately — the counselor makes the formal report. The Director coordinates and supports; does not independently file.

BrightLane Check-In System

A structured social-emotional check-in tool. The Director oversees enrollment, ensures check-ins are documented, and reviews data as part of the weekly wellness review.

Part Six

Title IX

The Director of Student Support and Culture serves as the Title IX Coordinator — responsible for procedural compliance, documentation integrity, and outcome communication.

Intake
Receive the complaint, document immediately, notify the Head of School, determine whether a formal investigation is triggered.
Investigation
Notify both parties of the process and their rights; gather witness statements one at a time; remain neutral throughout.
Documentation
Maintain an investigation report for every case; ensure record retention requirements are met.
Outcome
Draft outcome letters, identify safety measures, document resolution in the Title IX Tracker.
Non-negotiableEvery step must be documented. Title IX investigations with incomplete records create compliance exposure. If it is not documented, it did not happen.
Part Seven

Expulsion & Alternative Placement

Expulsion is a last resort — considered only when a student's behavior poses an ongoing safety risk that cannot be managed through MTSS, Critical Behavior Contracts have been exhausted, or a violation requires mandatory consideration under policy or law.

The Director reviews all MTSS documentation for fidelity, the Head of School must agree to proceed, families are notified in writing with hearing rights, and — for a student with an IEP — a Manifestation Determination Review must occur before any expulsion proceeds. While a student is on expulsion status, the Director coordinates temporary remote instruction, continued Aspire services, a parent communication schedule, and a re-entry plan.

Part Eight

Special Populations

McKinney-Vento

Immediate enrollment, services, and transportation for students lacking a fixed, regular, adequate nighttime residence. Any staff member who suspects a student qualifies notifies the Director and Social Worker within 24 hours; the District Liaison responds within 24–48 hours. Do not delay enrollment for missing documents.

Foster Care

Records transfer is expedited; a support meeting is scheduled within the first 2 weeks of enrollment; attendance is monitored closely and flagged immediately to the District Liaison.

Aspire Indiana

Behavioral planning and mental-health counseling for Tier 3 students. Parent consent is obtained before referral; collaboration meetings occur at least monthly for active Aspire students.

Missing / Withdrawn Students

Missing: notify leadership immediately, in-building sweep, then parent + IPS Police if not located within 10 minutes. Withdrawal: confirm the enrollment destination within 24 hours; escalate to the Head of School if unconfirmed after 5 school days.

Part Eleven

Documentation Standards

The non-negotiablePowerSchool is the official system of record. Documentation is not bureaucracy — it is the evidence of care. If it is not documented, it did not happen.

Every entry is accurate, objective, timely (within 24 hours), professional, and student-focused — observable behavior, actions taken, communication provided, intervention efforts, outcomes, and next steps. Never personal opinions, emotional language, labels, diagnoses, or speculation about family situations.
