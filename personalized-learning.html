Chapter 02
The Engine

Competency-Based Learning

Competency-Based Learning is how personalized learning comes to life at Matchbook K–8. It rests on four commitments — and a shared vocabulary.

Targeted Support

Students receive instruction based on what they need, not just what grade they are in.

Evidence Over Time

Learning is measured through demonstrated mastery, not seat time or completion.

Actionable Feedback

Students receive ongoing, specific feedback tied to clear success criteria.

Revision & Growth

Students always have the opportunity to revisit learning, receive support, and demonstrate mastery again.

Key Terms — the shared language of CBL
Competency
A clearly defined skill or knowledge area aligned to academic standards and student success outcomes.
Mastery
Consistent demonstration of understanding at the expected level — not perfection, but reliable, repeatable evidence.
Evidence of Learning
Student work that demonstrates mastery: projects, assessments, discussions, and teacher observations.
Formative Feedback
Ongoing, specific feedback that helps students understand strengths and identify clear next steps.
Reassessment
Structured opportunities to revisit learning, receive additional support, and demonstrate mastery again.

Chapter 04
Keeping It Accountable

Measuring the Work

To keep this vision from becoming an abstraction, the CBL Director’s work is organized around seven rubric domains — each weighted by its impact, each with an observable definition of strong practice.

Four performance levels — each domain is measured against the same scale, usable mid-year for a real-time check, not only at formal evaluation time.

Highly Effective

Practice is proactive, data-driven, and consistently reaches every student or teacher on schedule.

Effective

Practice happens on schedule and as planned, with regular review.

Improvement Necessary

Practice happens but is inconsistent, reactive, or not consistently tied to data.

Ineffective

Little or no evidence of the practice; not aligned to CBL implementation.

Chapter 06
How It Runs

The Operating Rhythm

The vision holds together because concrete routines and responsibilities keep it on schedule. Here is the operational backbone for the year.

Monthly Data Conferencing

  • Every student is conferenced each calendar month, tied to the Google Sheets progress tracker.
  • Grades K–6: teacher completes the Google Form during the conference. Grades 7–8: students complete it before the conference.
  • The Director of CBL monitors completion, follows up within three school days of any missed deadline, and produces a building-wide summary each semester for the Head of School.

Student-Led Conferences (Family-Facing)

  • Fall · Oct 8 (3–7 PM)Oct 9 (8 AM–3 PM)Spring · Mar 18 (3–7 PM)Mar 19 (8 AM–3 PM)
  • Every student leads the conference with their family and teacher, presenting evidence of growth across all five competencies.
  • Prep materials reach teachers no fewer than three weeks before each date; family materials are provided in English and Spanish.

Artifacts & Portfolios

  • Grades 7–8 digitize portfolios to UNRULR this year, aligning with The Match High School and preparing students for high school.
  • At least one grade-band pilot operates in semester one; the system expands in semester two. Teachers collect and submit artifacts on the established schedule.

Onboarding, PD & Framework Maintenance

  • A full-staff CBL session each August (before students arrive) covers the vision, the five competencies, framework updates, data deadlines, and the SLC calendar; PD continues throughout the year.
  • Definitions and K–8 progressions are reviewed each July and approved by the Head of School and Assistant Principal before distribution. Prior versions are archived, not deleted, to show the system’s growth.
  • Matchbook intends to partner more directly with Marzano Resources to build the competency system with fidelity.

Pathways & College/Career Readiness

  • Every 8th grader completes an annual pathway-identification process each spring, connected explicitly to competency growth and long-term outcomes.
  • CCR is taught in dedicated class time K–8: the Director of CBL teaches grades 7–8; the College & Career Teacher teaches grades K–6.

Family Communication Standards

  • All family-facing CBL materials are produced in English and Spanish and follow Matchbook brand standards (Century Gothic; red, black, gray).
  • The Director of CBL prepares at least one written progress update per semester; the Head of School receives a board-ready CBL update each month.
Chapter 07
The Why Behind It All

How We Live the Mission

Every element you just walked through exists to make three words real for every child.

Known

Every student has a teacher who sees them — not just their data, but their strengths, their story, and their potential. Conferences, small groups, and restorative relationships ensure no child falls through the cracks.

Loved

A restorative environment is a commitment to belonging. Students are held to high expectations within a culture of care, accountability, and second chances.

Flourishing

Flourishing is academic growth, resilience, and real-world readiness beyond test scores — excellence shown through projects, competencies, presentations, and habits students carry beyond Matchbook.

Strong instruction. Meaningful agency. Explicit MLL support.
Restorative structures. Real-world application. Future-ready students.
