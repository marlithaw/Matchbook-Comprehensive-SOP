Restorative Team & Counseling Operating Manual · 2026–27

Restorative & Counseling Handbook

Culture Engine · Wendell Phillips School 63

A role-based handbook for the Restorative Team and Counselors. Roles, responsibilities, and operating routines are defined here so the culture system holds steady across people, schedules, and daily pressure. All positions are full-time to these roles.

The one test: Does it prevent something that was preventable, or does it repair harm and restore a student to the community? If yes, it belongs in the Culture Engine.

Mission: Prevent the preventable. Build the conditions — regulation, relationship, belonging, and skill — that keep students in learning. When something breaks, repair quickly, restore dignity, and get students back where they belong.

The Commitments We Hold

These are not bullet points to memorize. They are what the team comes back to when the day gets hard.

Part 1

Part 2

Adult Regulation First

Restorative work begins with the adult. Before this team can help students regulate, repair, and re-enter the community, adults must be able to enter difficult moments with steadiness, dignity, and control over their own response. "Body first. Words second" applies to adults too.

Protected Adult Reset

A genuine, off-the-floor, duty-free period every day — protected, staggered, and non-negotiable. Skipping restoration is not proof of dedication; it weakens the capacity this work depends on.

Compassion Fatigue & Secondary Trauma

Staff use a brief weekly self-check on fatigue, dread, irritability, and depletion, and flag early. A daily team touch-base and standing check-in with the Director surface what is accumulating.

The Personal Pause (before responding)Stop at the threshold. Breathe — two or three slow, exhale-led breaths. Set the tone — soften face and voice, drop the shoulders. Check — "Am I regulated enough to regulate them?" If not, send the regulated colleague in first. Calm is contagious. So is alarm.
Part 3

The Proactive Engine

Prevention is where the Culture Engine does its deepest work — reaching students through systems every adult can run, so belonging is not limited to the students who generate referrals.

3.1 Connection Teams & Students-of-Concern

  • Build the list continuously from attendance, CICO trends, incident/repair data, and referral — updated weekly, not built once.
  • Every flagged student gets a sponsor / co-regulator adult.
  • Equity check: scan for over- or under-representation by group.

3.2 Check-In / Check-Out (CICO)

  • Morning check-in reads baseline window of tolerance and sets a simple goal.
  • Afternoon check-out celebrates what worked and sets tomorrow's target.
  • Progress tracked against clear entry/exit criteria.

3.3 Morning Meeting Participation

  • Teachers lead the routine; Restorative Team rotates through to model proactive presence.
  • Counselors contribute topic ideas after the first 90 days of onboarding — not a counseling-owned product.

3.4–3.6 Houses, Lunch Culture Loop & Adult Capacity

  • Houses deliver belonging: one owned recognition catalog (LiveSchool, Cardinal Character, Friday Cypher).
  • Lunch is Tier 1 prevention, not patrol — Regulate → Relate → Round.
  • Team rotates into classrooms to build adult capacity, not replace teacher routines.
Part 4

Live Classroom Intervention: Tap-In / Tap-Out

When a student is dysregulated, the default is not removal. The restorative member's job is to make the teacher's intervention possible — and to take the student out only when the moment requires a different adult first.

→
Tap-In · The Default (~5 min)

Restorative member pushes in and holds the class as a Warm Demander so the teacher steps into the hallway for a brief, private repair conversation, then both return. The teacher stays the intervener.

⇥
Tap-Out · The Exception

Used only when Tap-In is not in the student's or teacher's best interest — a safety concern, or the teacher is not regulated enough to engage. Bridges into the Regulation Room.

✓
Why the Teacher Leads

The rupture is usually between teacher and student; the relationship heals through the teacher, not around them. The restorative member holds the room so repair can happen with privacy and dignity.

Part 5

The Regulation Room — The 3Rs

One room, three zones. The work follows the order students need: regulate the body before relating, relate before reasoning. Framed explicitly as the Regulation Room to emphasize positive skill-building — never a punishment destination.

1
Regulate

Welcome neutrally, minimal words, flat tone, L-stance. Bottom-up sensory tools — water, weighted pads, breathing. No talking or demands until the body settles.

2
Relate

Once agitation settles, validate the student's emotional reality without validating the behavior. Adult connection creates felt safety.

3
Reason

Only after the prefrontal cortex is back online: who was harmed, what do they need, how do we make it right — co-create a matched-repair plan.

What It Is Not

The Regulation Room is not ISS, not a holding pen, not a punishment, and not an untracked space. Removal is not the intervention. The sequence is.

Part 6

Student Repair Process & Re-Entry

The purpose of "Reason" is real repair. The team scaffolds and arranges it; the student does it. The Student Repair Arc: Regulate → Reflect → Own it → Repair the impact → Re-enter → Follow through / close the loop.

Parts 7 – 8

Daily Operations, Coverage & Contingencies

Coverage protects two things at once: student safety and adult readiness. Two posts are never empty — a Room Lead and a Rapid Response post (★). Proactive work flexes only long enough to protect the spine, then is rebuilt.

C1 / C2Counselor — K–4 / 5–8

Coverage decision ladder1) Is anyone unsafe? 2) Is the Regulation Room covered? 3) Are CICO/DBRC and repair loops protected? 4) Which proactive routine can flex and be rebuilt later? 5) Has the loop been closed? This prevents the team from solving the loudest problem while leaving the most important post uncovered.

Down a Dean

A senior restorative staffer assumes a spine post; proactive routines run with fewer people that day.

Down a Restorative Staffer

That band's room visits route to the Room Lead; the other two split proactive coverage.

Down a Counselor

Remaining counselor covers crisis for both bands; clinical groups compress.

Emergency — Restorative Response Code

Rapid Response (★) responds immediately; available Dean or Director acts as Incident Commander. The band counselor handles the clinical lane in parallel, separate from physical-safety responders.

Parts 9 – 10

Data, Documentation & the Tier Map

Repair-conversation completion, CICO data, Regulation Room visits, recognition reach, attendance, belonging signals, and Students-of-Concern movement are tracked as carefully as incidents. Documentation uses observable language, enters within 24 hours, and stays open until repair is completed and logged.

Tier & Escalation Map
TierWhat It Includes
Tier 1 — UniversalMorning Meetings, Lunch Culture Loop, recognition and Houses, regulation tools, taught expectations. Owned by teachers with Restorative Team participation.
Tier 2 — TargetedCICO/DBRC, Connection Teams, small SEL/Behavior Skills groups, and the Regulation Room. Deans own consistency.
Tier 3 — IntensiveBIPs, FBAs, individualized plans, wraparound and Aspire, manifestation determinations. Clinical work led by counselors; decisions owned by the Director.
On ISS

The automatic "three Regulation Room referrals → ISS" rule is replaced by a Culture-Team decision based on data and an equity pause. If used, ISS is an exclusionary-coded last resort that follows the restorative sequence — never a Tier 2 entry point.

Appendix

Swap-In Tools

Short, printable tools referenced throughout the handbook — meant to live on a card, clipboard, or shared tracker.

Regulate · Entry

"I'm glad you're here. You don't have to talk. Have some water and take the time you need." — minimal words, flat tone, L-stance, sensory tools offered.

Relate · Validation Stems

"That makes sense that you felt ___." / "Anyone might feel ___ in that moment." — validate the feeling without endorsing the behavior.

Reason · Restorative Questions

What happened? Who was affected, and how? What were you needing? What would make it right? What will you do differently next time?

Re-Entry Sentence

"I'm ready to rejoin. I'm going to ___, and I've made it right by ___." Path: Correct → Repair → Rejoin.

Final Commitment

This handbook will shift as we learn. What will not shift: we prevent what is preventable, we regulate before we reason, and we never take away a student's place in this community. That is the work.
